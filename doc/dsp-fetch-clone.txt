Trusted pipeline snapshot transfer

DspFetchRun, DspFetchScheduledRun and McuDspFetchSchedule provide explicit
checkpoint constructors for fresh isolated native targets. They copy trusted
in-process native, frontend, operand, clock and timeline state. They do not
query or consume external input, execute instructions or alter source owners.
Callbacks and external event cursors are not part of the snapshot.

The DSP target must be unclocked, inactive and free of pending native work,
reset, faults and prior DMA cycles. The joint MCU target must additionally be
held at its fresh power-on boundary. The joint external binding must retain
its original identity, order, budget and callback presence. The original
nonzero MCU event floor is preserved. Input cursor consistency is checked on
subsequent execution, after the caller has restored its own control state.

Existing restore operations still require their original inactive owners and
contracts. Lifetime fences also distinguish a new producer occupying a retired
producer's address. Explicit transfer remains possible after the source owner
has been destroyed. A copied producer receives its own restore identity.

Construction may modify the disposable targets before a later failure. An
outer board must allocate and prepare the complete replacement, attach its
observers without invoking them, and publish native owners and control state
only after successful preparation. This API does not provide atomic rollback
of arbitrary existing targets or external I/O. MCU restore drops observers;
reattach them before execution. Host PCM and USB sessions require their own
existing lifecycle handling.

Directed native tests cover ten early fetch/decode/AG/E1/E2 cuts, independent
MOVE/CLR/ADD results and interleaved original/copy execution; joint held/running
MCU and DSP recovery states; initial oscillator phases; full native suffix
traces; distinct RAM writes; foreign and nonfresh admission; deferred stale
input refusal; and source destruction with exact native-address reuse.
The nonzero event-floor case starts after the actual POR/vector sequence.
ASan/UBSan and separate TSan exercise these single-threaded owners. They do
not establish concurrent access safety, full instrument boot or real-time
performance. Normal Board automatic pipeline integration remains open.
