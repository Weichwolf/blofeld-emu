Full-accumulator peripheral stores

The automatic_memory pipeline admits register-source MOVEP from full A/B to
the documented high and low X/Y short-I/O encodings. The predicate in
dsp_full_peripheral_store.hpp classifies only those 24-bit instruction words;
the original interpreter supplies the signed56-bit source, scaling, limiting,
24-bit bus value and actual flags. No host source value or new peripheral
handler substitutes for native execution. Raw A1/B1 has its separate scope in
dsp-middle-peripheral.txt; A0/A2/B0/B2 and control/stack stores remain unqualified.

The real F2 occurrence supplies the instruction through AG1/AG2 and native E1.
The existing source dependency interlock holds a consumer until the selected
older accumulator result is available. Partial/full writers and the opposite
accumulator retain their independently checked source masks. E1 writes the
actual native peripheral value once; E2 publishes genuine PC/counters/CCR.
Reserved scaling and unsupported CE/FV/SA/SC modes remain visible refusals.
REP-body admission and the SDK are unchanged.

Primary scope: DSP56300FM Rev5 sections3.1.3/3.5, Table12-13, MOVEP13-136
and AppendixA. The selected pipeline/publication order remains a reference
contract, not a measured DSP56371 sampling edge. Register-source MOVEP keeps
its nominal one instruction period plus actual interlocks. Known register
ownership is still required: reserved X:ffff80, unknown high-Y and other
unsupported ports do not become successful writes merely because the
instruction encoding is admitted.

The four own directed groups cover pp/split-qq fields, full A/B signed numeric
extrema and scale/limit flags, source-producer windows, accepted-code mutation,
checkpoint suffixes and fresh clones. The ESAI group samples two modeled
24-bit serial frames: an in-progress frame retains its older payload and the
next frame transmits the independently expected native value. This is not a
connected original voice chain or host audio stream.

Older peripheral fixtures distinguish mapped unknown F2 samples from their
actual use. A known older E1 write may complete while an unknown sample is
recorded. Its later required Decode fails without another clock or invented
instruction, preserving the actual older I/O. Later RAM writes cannot repair
that receipt. Trusted restore before F2 permits fresh known sampling and one
real write/retirement. Unsupported neighboring source forms remain guarded.
